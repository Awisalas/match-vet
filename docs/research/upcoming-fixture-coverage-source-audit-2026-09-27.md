# Upcoming fixture coverage source audit — 2026-09-27

This audit evaluates whether a no-cost source can lawfully and repeatably prove the upcoming fixture coverage required for MatchVet's seven target leagues. It is a research note for the discovered Product V2 live-acquisition gap; it does not change F01 or recommend accepting unproven coverage.

## Finding

As of 2026-09-27, I found **no source or source combination approved under MatchVet's reviewed use policy that both documents automated access and the retention needed by F03/T17 and proves complete date-bounded coverage for the requested Fixture Scope across all seven leagues**. This is a research finding, not a legal opinion about unreviewed rights. OpenFootAPI is a newly identified, more promising access/retention candidate: its terms explicitly permit indefinite caching and internal research at the API-operator level, and its current competition catalog lists all seven 2026/27 target competitions with fixture capability and per-competition source/license labels. But the labels do not establish every upstream permission equally, and no reviewed candidate has a documented coverage contract or explicit provider metadata that establishes the entire requested scope, including an authoritative empty result. Date filters, season lists, a total-page count, or language such as “all matches” describe what the provider returns, not whether it has the entire real-world schedule.

OpenFootAPI's catalog lists all seven target competitions with fixture capability for 2026/27; its documented free Starter plan includes fixtures, and its terms permit local retention and internal research at the API-operator level. However, its current API version is beta, its OpenAPI description caps the public preview at five matches and says cursor pagination requires the paid Developer plan, its upstream source metadata includes an unclear `Unlicensed-Beta (owner-approved factual data)` label for five leagues, and its terms say particular match availability is not guaranteed and upstream data can be incomplete. Thus it is a practical row source to investigate further, but not a source of F01 proof. API-Football also lists all seven but has weaker retention/underlying-rights terms. Football-data.org's free tier covers six target leagues, not Belgium, and supplies no completeness contract. OpenFootball supplies useful 2026/27 rows for six leagues under the repository CC0 notice, but that notice does not clear third-party source rights; its manually maintained upstream has no update guarantee, no completeness/empty metadata, and shares lineage between its Football.TXT input and generated JSON. No reviewed source emits qualifying F01 evidence today.

Belgium is the clearest blocker. The Pro League's public site says exact match times are fixed through after the winter break, and the next calendar update is scheduled for 8 December. That makes schedule content available for current Matchweeks, but the reviewed public material gives no documented machine feed or permission for automated capture and local retention. Football-data.org lists Jupiler Pro League outside its free tier. OpenFootball's 2026/27 Belgium text file still has exact dates only through round 7 and marks rounds 8–34 TBC; it is a separate repository with shared credited upstream sources, not independent corroboration.

**Product consequence at audit time:** preserve F01's exact noncomplete state (`UNKNOWN`, `PARTIAL`, or `STALE`) and F06's `ASSESSMENT_INCOMPLETE` refusal wherever full current proof is missing. The 2026-09-27 recommendation was to keep a source-contract/product-decision blocker rather than create an implementation-ready provider ticket. The product owner's 2026-09-28 decision is recorded below and selects the temporary Research-Only attestation bridge; it does not change this research finding or lower F01's proof standard.

## The proof test

The requested scope is each league and season over one Matchweek window. The existing [MatchweekWindow](../../src/matchvet/matchweek.py) is Friday inclusive through Tuesday exclusive in Africa/Lagos, covering Friday–Monday. F01's [ProviderCoverageEvidence](../../src/matchvet/fixture_coverage.py) accepts only a capture tied to a captured attempt by ID and digest, with the exact competition and season, a non-null coverage basis, source-backed UTC bounds, a permitted-use policy, current freshness, complete declared partition accounting, and exhausted pagination. The coverage ranges must cover the whole requested window. For `CONFIRMED_EMPTY`, the whole range must additionally be affirmatively declared empty. `COMPLETE` requires at least one resolved fixture identity and full-window coverage.

For every claimed provider path, the audit applies the required question:

> **Exactly what proves this provider covered the entire requested Fixture Scope?**

An HTTP 200, valid JSON/CSV, many rows, min/max observed dates, an existing season file, a query filter matching the requested dates, or “no rows returned” answers only what the captured response contained. A pagination total proves only that the pages reported by that response were fetched. A provider can create F01 evidence only if a versioned provider contract or explicit response metadata says the queried competition/season/date bounds are exhaustively covered, identifies all required partitions/pages, supplies source-as-of/freshness semantics, and explicitly distinguishes an empty covered scope from missing/unsupported data. The reviewed candidates do not provide that proof.

## Candidate provider review

All live and documentation observations below were checked on 2026-09-27. “Not found” means the reviewed primary documentation and terms did not establish the listed permission or semantics; it is not a legal opinion that no permission could be obtained directly from the rights holder.

### Football-data.org API

The provider's [coverage page](https://www.football-data.org/coverage) lists Premier League, Serie A, La Liga, Bundesliga, Ligue 1, and Primeira Liga in its free tier. Jupiler Pro League appears in the all-competitions list but not under Free Tier. The API is documented, uses an account token, and supports competition match lists with season and date filters, offset/limit pagination, and a `resultSet` containing count and first/last dates ([match resource](https://docs.football-data.org/general/v4/match.html), [resource examples](https://docs.football-data.org/general/v4/resources.html), [filters](https://docs.football-data.org/general/v4/lookup_tables.html)). A result count and date range describe records in the provider response; no reviewed contract says they are the number/bounds of every real fixture for the requested schedule scope. There is no documented schedule snapshot/as-of field or affirmative-empty declaration.

Free access for six leagues and documented query mechanics make this a potentially useful observation source. They do not answer “Exactly what proves this provider covered the entire requested Fixture Scope?” The docs define neither a complete schedule guarantee nor exhaustive response semantics. Its [about/terms page](https://www.football-data.org/about) requires attribution, disclaims accuracy/reliability/availability guarantees, and says fixture/result data cannot continue to be referenced after subscription cancellation. We found no explicit durable raw-capture retention license. It cannot currently establish F01 coverage and cannot cover all seven at zero cost.

### API-Football (API-Sports)

The [coverage page](https://www.api-football.com/coverage), checked 2026-09-27, lists fixture coverage for all seven targets, including Belgium's Jupiler Pro League. The [pricing page](https://www.api-football.com/pricing) advertises a $0 tier with 100 requests/day; it has limits on available seasons. The documented fixtures API accepts league, season, date range, or round filters; responses expose pagination totals ([fixture guide](https://www.api-football.com/news/post/how-to-get-started-with-api-football-the-complete-beginners-guide), [quota/pagination guide](https://www.api-football.com/news/post/how-to-optimize-api-sports-calls-and-quota-usage)). Its guide describes querying a season and retrieving all pages, but its [coverage page](https://www.api-football.com/coverage) warns that coverage can vary by season and fixtures and that availability does not guarantee availability in the terms.

The terms say data are provided “as is,” with no guarantee; update frequency is indicative and not guaranteed. They also say API-Football does not grant a license to use or publish data in user products and that users are responsible for obtaining necessary permissions from the competent authorities/rights holders ([terms](https://www.api-football.com/terms)). Resale is prohibited. We found no raw-snapshot retention duration or third-party rights clearance covering all seven competitions. This is the broadest practical row source, but its “all fixtures” / page-total behavior proves only what its database returned. It has no contractual whole-scope or affirmative-empty proof and does not meet the rights bar for retained MatchVet research data.

### OpenFootAPI

OpenFootAPI is the strongest newly identified zero-cost candidate for a single all-seven acquisition adapter. Its current [coverage catalog](https://openfootapi.com/coverage) lists 2026/27 fixtures for every target. A read of the documented [GET `/v1/competitions`](https://openfootapi.com/v1/competitions) endpoint on 2026-09-27 (using the public demo credential shown in the docs, for inspection only) returned `fixtures: true`, 2026/27 as the current season, source identity, and a license label for each target. The observed API response metadata included `generated_at` and `request_id`, but no upstream `source_as_of`. The mapping was:

OpenFootAPI's [pricing page](https://openfootapi.com/pricing) lists Starter at $0/month with 5,000 authenticated requests/month and fixture/schedule access across its catalog; it documents a 60-request/minute rate limit. The [docs](https://openfootapi.com/docs) expose filters by competition, season, and UTC date. The published [OpenAPI 3.1 spec](https://openfootapi.com/openapi.json) identifies version `1.0.0-beta`, says the public preview returns at most five matches, and says cursor pagination (up to 100 matches/page) requires Developer access. The free Starter plan's pricing page promises fixture access but the reviewed OpenAPI does not document a Starter cursor or another exhaustive paging mechanism. A five-match preview cannot be assumed complete for a league Matchweek; splitting by date is not a guarantee that each day has five or fewer matches. Even if a paid client fetched every cursor page, that would prove only that all rows reported by OpenFootAPI were read, not that the upstream schedule is complete.

**Rights and retention:** OpenFootAPI's [terms, §7](https://openfootapi.com/terms) expressly allow caching, normalized-field storage, exact-response storage, derived aggregates, hashes, and audit records without a maximum retention period; they allow internal statistical research, model training, evaluation, and validation, including after service access ends. This is a meaningful permission from the API operator for MatchVet's non-redistributive research use. If data are published, §7 says to carry through the source attribution; it also says that using a competition is not a license to redistribute its underlying feed and that each source's obligations attach directly. Section 8 says third-party materials remain subject to their owners' rights. The five FotMob entries' `Unlicensed-Beta (owner-approved factual data)` string is not a published license with enough detail to independently establish the scope of that permission. **Is the `Unlicensed-Beta (owner-approved factual data)` metadata, together with OpenFootAPI §7's internal-research grant, enough to grant MatchVet a right to retain those five upstream datasets internally?** The public terms do not resolve whether the asserted approval covers this use, bulk retrieval, or indefinite retention. Keep those five leagues unapproved until OpenFootAPI identifies the permission scope or provides the underlying written rights statement. For Liga Portugal and Belgium, the source metadata points to Football-Data.co.uk, whose current official terms limit access to private individuals, disallow commercial or data-training products using automated bots/scrapers/AI, and describe the data as for league-match prediction. OpenFootAPI's `Free-Open-Data` label and generic internal-research grant cannot override that upstream restriction; require clarification that MatchVet's automated acquisition/retention is allowed before approval.

**Completeness and freshness:** `fixtures: true`, `availableSeasons`, `meta.count`, `meta.generated_at`, source/license metadata, and date/season filters do not claim the service has every fixture in a requested scope. The terms say source availability and freshness vary, data may be delayed, incomplete, corrected, or unavailable, and no particular match/competition is guaranteed ([terms §§2, 5](https://openfootapi.com/terms)). No response field or versioned source contract declares complete bounds or affirmatively empty scope. `generated_at` timestamps when OpenFootAPI generated its response, not when its upstream schedule was last checked. Therefore the precise answer to **“Exactly what proves this provider covered the entire requested Fixture Scope?”** is: nothing in the reviewed contract or metadata. `fixtures: true` establishes only a capability flag, and a returned page/cursor set can establish only exhaustion of the returned API result. A zero-result response cannot establish `CONFIRMED_EMPTY`.

The OpenAPI schema also defines `meta.scope.mode`, `partial`, `competitions_searched`, and `competitions_total`, plus `meta.unavailable` entries; the only currently enumerated unavailable reason is `season_not_published_upstream` ([OpenAPI spec](https://openfootapi.com/openapi.json)). These are useful diagnostics: they can describe which competitions the service searched or explain that an upstream season was unavailable. The schema does not define `partial: false` or `competitions_searched == competitions_total` as an attestation that the upstream schedule contains every real fixture in requested bounds. Nor does an `unavailable` reason assert that a supported, exhaustively checked scope is empty; it explicitly signals unavailable upstream data. None supplies an upstream source-as-of, exhaustive fixture partitions, stable complete snapshot, or authoritative empty-scope assertion. These fields therefore cannot close the F01 proof gap.

To distinguish these diagnostics from a completeness contract, a read-only probe used only the documented [public demo key and API](https://openfootapi.com/docs) on 2026-09-27 around 20:04 UTC. `GET /v1/matches?competition=comp_pro_league_be&season=2026/27` returned `count: 5`, `total_count: 63`, `preview_limit: 5`, and a non-null `pagination.next_cursor`, but `meta.scope.partial` was `false`; `meta.unavailable` was empty. Thus `partial: false` did not mean all filtered rows were returned, much less that the source's 63 rows were the complete real-world season. This filtered response showed the request filters in `meta.filters` and did not include `meta.scope.window`. `GET /v1/matches?date=2026-08-28` returned a five-row preview, a next cursor, `partial: true`, and three unavailable competition entries with reason `season_not_published_upstream`. That reason distinguishes an unserved upstream season from a normal response, but does not declare an empty fixture scope. The spec documents the default unfiltered window as today ±3 days UTC; a supplied filter replaces that default window with the caller's requested filter, but does not state that the provider has exhaustively sourced all fixtures matching it. The observed responses included `generated_at`, not upstream `source_as_of`. The demo key is documented for testing, is rate-limited, and is not intended for production use; the free preview remains capped at five results while cursor pagination requires Developer access.

The same live demo `/v1/competitions` response returned 120 catalog entries and nine names under `meta.sources`. The published OpenAPI `SourceAttribution.name` enum allows `OpenLigaDB`, `OpenFootball`, `Football-Data.co.uk`, `Understat`, and `ESPN`; the live aggregate also included `FotMob`, `LPF`, `Livesport`, and `TheSportsDB`. Those four `meta.sources[].name` values violate the current enum. Individual `data[].source.name` is a free-form string in the schema, so FotMob or Football-Data.co.uk labels in per-competition source objects are not themselves enum violations. The conformance mismatch makes aggregate attribution handling brittle but does not provide completeness evidence. The `/v1/competitions` response had `generated_at: 2026-09-27T20:03:31Z` and no `scope` or `unavailable` fields; the spec associates those diagnostics with `/v1/matches` behavior.

**Maintenance and lineage:** The spec calls itself an open-data beta and says no uptime SLA is included. The terms permit features, upstreams, and coverage to change or be suspended. The docs carry a September 2026 migration notice describing changed match-ID prefixes and retired OpenFootball IDs, so consumers need to expect contract/source churn. Per-competition source metadata currently identifies FotMob for the five top-five leagues; the docs separately identify OpenFootball CC0 as an archive source, not a second independent current fixture source. Liga Portugal and Belgian Pro League are mapped to Football-Data.co.uk, so those entries share lineage with MatchVet's existing Football-Data.co.uk observations and cannot count as independent corroboration. No beta/API-health endpoint can supply completeness evidence unless it also states the covered fixture scope.

### OpenFootball JSON and Football.TXT

The [football.json README](https://github.com/openfootball/football.json/blob/master/README.md) identifies the files as public-domain fixture/result datasets and describes a JSON `matches` array. It says latest-season JSON files are generated once daily at 05:00 UTC from upstream Football.TXT via GitHub Action, but the upstream text datasets are not automatically updated daily. The [help repository](https://github.com/openfootball/help) describes the datasets as wiki-like, community-maintained files. There is no service snapshot/as-of field, freshness guarantee, date-bound coverage declaration, pagination contract, or affirmative-empty metadata. A season file is not a proof that every requested date is represented.

The 2026/27 JSON directory contains six target feeds: [England](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openfootball/football.json/master/2026-27/en.1.json), [Italy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openfootball/football.json/master/2026-27/it.1.json), [Spain](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openfootball/football.json/master/2026-27/es.1.json), [Germany](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openfootball/football.json/master/2026-27/de.1.json), [France](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openfootball/football.json/master/2026-27/fr.1.json), and [Portugal](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openfootball/football.json/master/2026-27/pt.1.json); there is no Belgium JSON file in the [2026/27 directory](https://github.com/openfootball/football.json/tree/master/2026-27). The generated JSON and upstream Football.TXT are one lineage, not independent sources. The [CC0 notice](https://github.com/openfootball/football.json/blob/master/LICENSE.md) allows broad reuse of repository contributions, but expressly disclaims warranties and does not clear third-party rights. That matters because the [Belgium source file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openfootball/belgium/master/2026-27/be1.txt) credits Pro League and VoetbalPrimeur. This makes the six JSON feeds useful local fixture observations, not valid evidence of coverage or an automatically cleared schedule license.

The Belgium Football.TXT file lists 34 rounds and 18 clubs but, as fetched 2026-09-27, only assigns exact dates/times through round 7; rounds 8–34 are TBC. Its repository license does not prove that the source credited in the file permits automated capture or local retention. Because the published official Belgian calendar now has more precise dates than this file, the TXT's staleness also illustrates why daily regeneration of derived JSON does not imply daily freshness of underlying fixture data.

### Football-Data.co.uk

This is distinct from Football-data.org. Its [data page](https://www.football-data.co.uk/data.php) offers free downloadable match CSVs for league-match prediction; its [weekly fixtures page](https://www.football-data.co.uk/matches.php) describes a short-horizon fixture/odds feed. In the repository's 2026-09-23 capture, all seven 2026/27 season CSVs had rows but zero future unscored fixtures; the separate weekly CSV ended on 2026-09-20 and the page's latest upload was 2026-09-18 ([prior live capture and row counts](upcoming-fixture-provider-capability-2026-09-23.md)). Neither route publishes coverage bounds, completeness, as-of metadata, or affirmative-empty semantics. The weekly file also carries betting odds, which are outside the fixture contract. The provider's use statement is narrow, and these CSVs cannot prove F01 coverage.

### TheSportsDB and OpenLigaDB

TheSportsDB's [API documentation](https://www.thesportsdb.com/documentation) describes a free V1 season endpoint (`eventsseason`) with a free response limit of 15 and no documented paging mechanism for that endpoint; the larger season schedule endpoint is premium. A live EPL 2026/27 request returned 15 events, which does not prove either that only 15 exist or that the first 15 are the whole schedule. The provider identifies itself as a crowd-sourced database ([about](https://www.thesportsdb.com/docs_about)). Its [terms](https://www.thesportsdb.com/docs_terms_of_use.php) allow API endpoint data use under stated restrictions but do not grant rights in third-party content generally; app-store and image-right restrictions apply. There is no source-as-of, completeness, partition, or empty-scope contract. It is not a proof source.

OpenLigaDB exposes a no-authentication API with an ODbL data license, a 60-request/minute/IP limit, whole-season query, available-groups enumeration, and per-group last-change timestamps ([Swagger API docs](https://api.openligadb.de/index.html), [project and terms](https://www.openligadb.de/)). Its `/getmatchdata/{leagueShortcut}/{leagueSeason}` endpoint is documented as returning all match data the database holds for that league/season. Enumerating the groups and fetching each page/group could prove the database query was exhausted, but there is no provider assertion that its community-entered database contains the entire actual schedule, no complete-window empty declaration, and no whole-schedule as-of value. Per-group last-change timestamps do not prove all groups are up to date. The live [2026 index](https://api.openligadb.de/getavailableleagues/2026) checked on 2026-09-27 exposed entries for Premier League, LaLiga, and Bundesliga, but not Belgium, Serie A, Ligue 1, or Liga Portugal (the index itself includes various duplicate/test entries, so this is a database listing observation only). ODbL carries attribution/share-alike obligations; it does not turn community records into an authoritative fixture contract.

## Freshness, empty results, and source rights

- **Freshness:** F01 requires an externally evaluated, versioned freshness policy, but the source must provide a meaningful source-as-of/update marker or contractual update semantics for that policy to evaluate. API retrieval time only shows when MatchVet asked. OpenFootAPI's `generated_at` is response-generation time and its observed competition entry has no upstream `source_as_of`; its terms say freshness varies and data may be delayed or incomplete. OpenFootball's daily JSON generation timestamp is not the upstream schedule's last revision; its source data is manual. OpenLigaDB's per-group changed timestamp only dates edits to that DB group, not source currency. API-Football says update-frequency information is indicative and not guaranteed. Official PL calendar says it updates after changes, but is not a documented reusable machine feed. No reviewed source provides the needed reliable whole-schedule as-of guarantee.
- **Pagination/partitioning:** A provider's `total` page count or `offset` tells the client when it has read every response page reported by that service. It does not establish the service captured every scheduled event. An accepted contract would need to define each required partition (for example competition + season + requested UTC interval), required pages/cursors, terminal pagination signal, and consistency rules while fetching; evidence must account for all of them. A response with no `next` page or no groups is not affirmative-empty evidence by itself.
- **Affirmative empty:** No reviewed source supplies explicit metadata asserting that a supported competition, season, and bounded requested interval has been exhaustively checked and is empty. Zero rows must therefore remain `UNKNOWN`, even if the query succeeded and every reported page was read.
- **Rights and retention:** OpenFootAPI is the only reviewed API whose terms explicitly allow unlimited response retention and internal research; that permission is subject to the source-specific rights it explicitly does not waive. Its Football-Data.co.uk entries carry a vendor `Free-Open-Data` label, but the upstream page restricts automated bot/scraper/AI products, leaving MatchVet's method unresolved; do not treat the label as permission. Its FotMob entries have an unresolved `Unlicensed-Beta (owner-approved factual data)` label. OpenFootball CC0 permits reuse of repository contributions but cannot clear third-party schedule sources credited by contributors. OpenLigaDB documents ODbL with attribution/share-alike duties, but its crowdsourced DB lacks completeness/authority. Football-data.org's free tier requires attribution, but its docs/terms provide no durable local snapshot license or coverage warranty. API-Football expressly leaves rights clearance to the user and grants no data publication license; it does not give adequate underlying schedule rights/retention assurance for this purpose. Official league pages often have full calendars, but their terms restrict automated access, reuse, retention, or database creation, with the Bundesliga explicitly barring bots. No candidate satisfies both proof and use policy.
- **Current registry alignment:** The active `_SOURCE_RIGHTS` mapping in `src/matchvet/ingestion.py` labels OpenFootball JSON and Football.TXT `CC0`, `RETAIN_REUSABLE`, and redistributable. F05 maps `CC0` to `PERMITTED`. The public-domain/CC0 notice applies to rights the contributor owns and expressly does not clear third-party schedule rights; the active policy therefore needs a rights-chain review before it is relied on for retained/exportable observations or new coverage evidence. Football-Data.co.uk is currently registered as restricted private use and F05 maps that category to `PERMITTED`; verify that this policy exactly fits the current automated, private research use, upstream conditions, and storage/backup behavior. Do not rewrite historical records in place. Any changed conclusion needs a versioned policy and an explicit treatment of new captures, existing artifacts, exports, and backups.
- **Distribution/backup:** Any later implementation ticket must register provider identity, upstream lineage, exact provider-use policy revision, attribution/license duties, and the data representation permitted for durable local storage. The exact capture representation referenced by F01/F03 must remain available and verifiable through existing T17 backup/replay. If terms allow only citation or data that cannot satisfy that persistence contract, that source cannot provide F01 evidence under the current model; changing that contract requires a separately versioned persistence design. Do not omit a capture from backup while retaining F01/F03 references to it. CC0-derived and ODbL-derived captures need distinct policies and lineage; do not merge shared-lineage material as independent corroboration.
